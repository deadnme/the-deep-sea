The last of the light.

Twilight zone, mesopelagic, to 1,000 m

Sunlight fades to a dim blue, too weak for plants to grow. Many animals here make their own light, and each night billions of them swim up to feed.

Ahmed Gabr, 2014. Getting down took minutes. Decompressing on the way back up took about 14 hours.

Midnight zone, bathypelagic, to 4,000 m

No sunlight reaches this deep. From here down, the only light is made by animals, like the glowing lure an anglerfish dangles in front of its jaws.

Those pale specks are marine snow: dead plankton, droppings and dust, sinking from above. Some flakes fall for weeks, and many deep-sea animals live on it.

Cold, dark and crushing.

Abyssal zone, abyssopelagic, to 6,000 m

The water is near freezing and the pressure is hundreds of times that at the surface. Life here waits for food to fall from above.

4,790 m

Bismarck

The German battleship sank in 1941. Its wreck was found in 1989.

Abyssal plains, flat fields of fine sediment, cover about half of Earth's surface. Almost none of it has ever been seen.

Into the trenches.

Hadal zone, hadopelagic, to the floor

Named after Hades, this zone exists only inside deep ocean trenches. The walls close in on both sides.

Alvin's limit

6,865 m

Deepest known shipwreck

USS Samuel B. Roberts, sunk in 1944 and found in 2022.

8,200 m

The fish limit

Around here, fish seem to reach the edge of what their bodies can take. Below it, amphipods take over.

    About this site

    A little context.

    Every metre of depth gets the same amount of scrolling, so the long, empty stretches really are that long. Animals sit at depths where they are found, though most range much wider. Their shapes and sizes are stylized and not to scale.

    Pressure is estimated at one atmosphere per 10 metres plus surface pressure. The floor uses NOAA's approximate depth; measurements of the Challenger Deep vary. No sunlight reaches below about 1,000 m, so creatures there are lit for you to see them. Marine snow is drawn thicker with depth so it shows up in the dark.
